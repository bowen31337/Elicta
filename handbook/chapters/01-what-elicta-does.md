# What Elicta Does

Imagine a meeting where a client is describing what they want built. Somebody
says *"we need the reports to be fast."*

That sounds like a requirement. It is not one. Fast for whom? Compared to what
they have now? A second, a minute, overnight? You almost certainly know to ask —
and quite often do not, because at that moment you are also listening, taking
notes, watching the clock, keeping the room comfortable, and trying to remember
which topics you have not covered yet.

So the moment passes. "Fast" goes into the notes as though it meant something.
Months later somebody builds a report that takes four seconds, the client says
that is not what they asked for, and it gets built again.

Elicta watches for that moment and puts the question in front of you while there
is still time to ask it.

![A suggestion, with the short version you can read at a glance and the reason it fired underneath](../../docs/journeys/screenshots/panel-nudge-surfaced.png)

## The shape of it

```diagram
type: steps
title: What you do, and what Elicta does
caption: The thinking is done in the first stage. The meeting only picks from work already finished.
item: Days before | You tell it about the client and give it what you already have. It drafts the questions worth asking. You read them, reorder them, and delete the ones that miss.
item: In the room | It listens. When something is left vague or a topic gets skipped, one suggested question appears. You decide whether to ask it, with a single tap.
item: Afterwards | It writes up what was agreed — the open questions, the decisions, a project brief, a follow-up email — with the words that were actually said attached to every claim.
```

## What it is like to use

It is quiet. A good meeting might produce three suggestions, not twelve. When it
is unsure, it says nothing, because one suggestion that embarrasses you in front
of a client costs far more than a good one earns.

Every response is a single tap. There is nothing to type, nothing to read
through, and no conversation to have with it. It offers; you accept or you do
not.

And it never speaks to the client. It has no voice in the meeting, sends nothing
on your behalf, and joins nothing as a participant that talks. If you ignore it
for ninety minutes, nothing happens — that is a supported way to use it.

## Who this guide is for

The person running the meeting, and whoever sets Elicta up for them. It covers
what each screen does and how to use it, in order: setting it up, preparing for
an engagement, the meeting itself, and what comes out afterwards.

It assumes nothing about how the software is built, and nothing about the
company being interviewed. Where something is not finished yet, this guide says
so rather than describing what is intended as though it already worked.
