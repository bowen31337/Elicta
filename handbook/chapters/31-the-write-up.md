# Getting the Write-Up

The meeting produces a transcript. What you actually need is a set of documents
somebody can act on, where every claim can be traced back to the moment it came
from.

## What comes out

```diagram
type: steps
title: The documents
item: The open questions | Ranked by how much they matter to the build, so the list is a plan rather than an inventory.
item: A log of decisions | What was settled, and by whom. This is the part people forget and then argue about.
item: A draft project brief | What is being built, for whom, and why — assembled from what was said rather than from a template.
item: A follow-up email | Covering what you heard and what you still need, for you to read and send. It is never sent for you.
```

![The write-up, with every claim showing where it came from](../../docs/journeys/screenshots/debrief-artifacts.png)

## Said, or worked out

This is the distinction a reviewer cares about most, so it is on the face of
every claim rather than buried in it.

```diagram
type: compare
title: Two kinds of claim, always labelled
left: Stated
right: Inferred
item: Where it came from | The client said it | Elicta concluded it
item: What is attached | The quote, with who said it and when | The quote it was concluded from
item: What you do with it | Check the wording | Judge whether you agree
```

Blurring those two would make the whole document untrustworthy, so nothing can be
written without a source attached. It is not a habit or a review step: a claim
without a source cannot be saved at all.

## Asking about the meeting in your own words

The documents answer the questions somebody anticipated. For the ones nobody did
— *what did they actually say about the March deadline*, *draft me the paragraph
about integrations* — you can just ask.

Before you have asked anything, the screen offers a few openers and says plainly
where its answers come from.

![The conversation before anything has been asked](../../docs/journeys/screenshots/debrief-conversation-start.png)

![Asking a question about the meeting in your own words](../../docs/journeys/screenshots/debrief-conversation.png)

Answers quote and attribute rather than summarise, and say plainly when something
was worked out rather than said. That is the same rule the documents follow, for
the same reason: an answer you cannot check is one you have to take on trust, and
this is not a product that asks for trust.

## If something goes wrong partway

The write-up runs as a sequence of steps. If one of them fails, the sequence stops
there and says so, rather than passing invented material to the next step.

You get a partial result and an explanation, which is recoverable. You do not get
a complete-looking document with a fabricated section in it, which is not.

## Where this is honest about itself

The whole sequence works, and so does asking follow-up questions in your own
words. Producing a complete run needs one of the outside services with enough
capacity; the connection has been tested against the live service and works, but
the account used for testing was rate limited, so a full run has not yet been
produced end to end.
