# What It Will Not Do

It is easier to trust a tool when you know where its edges are. This is the list
of things Elicta deliberately does not do, and why each line was drawn.

## It never speaks to the client

It has no voice in the meeting. It does not send messages, and it does not act on
anybody's behalf. Every suggestion is shown to you, and you decide.

If you ignore it for ninety minutes, nothing happens. That is a supported way to
use it, not a failure.

## It is not a transcription product

It does turn speech into text, because it cannot work otherwise. But that part is
bought in, and it is not what Elicta is for. If all you want is a recording and a
transcript, this is a great deal of machinery for something simpler tools already
do well.

## It is not a note-taker

Notes come out of it, as a by-product. The purpose is the question asked in the
room. A tool that captured the meeting perfectly and never improved what was
asked would have missed the point entirely.

## It is not something you talk to during a meeting

You cannot ask it things while the meeting is running. There is no conversation
with it. It offers; you accept or decline.

That restriction is deliberate. Anything that invites you to start typing has
taken your attention away from the client, which is the exact problem it was
built to relieve. Asking it things in your own words is something you do
afterwards, on a different screen.

## It stays quiet when it is unsure

Given the choice between a suggestion that might be useful and saying nothing, it
says nothing.

One suggestion that embarrasses you in front of a client does more damage than
ten good ones repair. So the bar for speaking is set high, and it is checked
against recordings of real meetings before every release.

The practical effect is that Elicta will sometimes miss something a sharp analyst
would have caught. That trade was made knowingly, and it is the right way round.

## It does not replace judgement

It does not decide what matters, what is worth pushing on, or when to let
something go because the room has had enough.

It notices, it suggests, and it writes down what it heard. Everything that
requires knowing this client, this project and this moment stays with you.
